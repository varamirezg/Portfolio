About Me

Where engineering meets business and innovation

I am a dual-degree graduate in Production Engineering and Geology, currently pursuing a Master's degree in Management Engineering at Politecnico di Milano, where I am strengthening my expertise at the intersection of technology, business strategy, and innovation. This foundation has shaped my interest in bridging scientific and technological innovation with business applications, particularly within the space economy, environmental technologies, and digital transformation.

My current interests include artificial intelligence, machine learning, predictive analytics, digital business innovation, and data-driven marketing. I am particularly interested in exploring how satellite data, Earth observation technologies, and intelligent analytical models can create new business opportunities, improve strategic decision-making, and support sustainable development. I am also passionate about the management of complex, multidisciplinary projects, particularly those integrating advanced technologies, environmental systems, space applications, and Earth sciences.

I aim to combine technical expertise with strategic and managerial capabilities to translate scientific insights into commercially viable, innovative, and impactful solutions. With strong analytical thinking, adaptability, leadership, and a multidisciplinary perspective, my career ambition is to contribute to technology-driven businesses and innovative projects that connect science, engineering, and management, creating value across industries while addressing global environmental and technological challenges.

Hydrology · Remote Sensing

CHIRPS vs. Rain Gauges - Tatacoa Desert

Multitemporal comparative analysis of precipitation between the CHIRPS satellite product and rain gauge stations in the Tatacoa Desert, a tropical dry forest in the northern Andes and the second driest area of Colombia. Since groundwater is often the main drinking-water source and the Neiva-Tatacoa-Garzón aquifer system lacks sufficient data, validating satellite precipitation is essential before using it in soil-water-balance recharge estimation. Validated CHIRPS at daily, monthly, and annual scales from 1981 to 2021 against eleven rain gauge stations.
